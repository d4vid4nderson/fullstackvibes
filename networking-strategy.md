### Day 3-5: The Reconnection Strategy

**Pick 3 people from your list and send them a message**

**Template 1: Former Colleagues Who Left MOREgroup**

```
Hey [Name],

I saw you moved to [Company] - congrats! I've been thinking about making a move myself after 12 years at MOREgroup.

I'd love to hear about your experience making the transition. Would you be open to a 20-minute call sometime? I'm specifically curious about [how you found the role / what made you decide to leave / how you positioned your MOREgroup experience].

No pressure if you're swamped. Either way, hope you're doing well.

David
```

**Why this works:**
- Genuine reason to reach out (you're both MOREgroup alumni)
- Specific ask (20 minutes, clear topic)
- Easy to say yes to
- Not asking for a job

---

**Template 2: Vendors/Partners You've Worked With**


- You're acknowledging their expertise
- Shows you're serious about exploring

### What to Say on the Calls

**First 5 minutes:** Build rapport
- Thank them for their time
- Quick intro about yourself (2 minutes)
- Ask them about their background

**Next 10 minutes:** Ask good questions
- "How did you end up in your current role?"
- "What skills from your previous role transferred well?"
- "What surprised you about the transition?"
- "What do you wish you'd known before making the move?"
- "What's the hiring market like in [their space]?"

**Last 5 minutes:** The ask
- "Based on what I've told you, what roles do you think would be a good fit?"
- "Do you know anyone else I should talk to?"
- "Would you be open to introducing me to [specific person/company]?"
- "Can I follow up in a few weeks and share how things are going?"

**After the call:**
- Send thank you email within 24 hours
- Connect on LinkedIn if you haven't
- Actually follow up in 2-3 weeks with an update
- Offer to help them with something (introduction, advice, whatever)

## Week 9-12: Targeted Applications + Warm Introductions

### Goal: Apply to 12 roles (1/week) with warm introductions

**Stop:** Spray and pray applications
**Start:** Targeted applications with context

**The Strategy:**

**Step 1: Find companies you actually want to work for**

Make a list of 20 companies:
- AI startups you admire
- SaaS companies doing interesting work
- Companies building products you'd use
- Companies with remote-first culture

**Research:**
- What products do they build?
- What's their tech stack?
- Who's on their product/engineering team?
- Are they hiring?

**Step 2: Find a connection to each company**

For each company, check:
- Do I have a 1st degree LinkedIn connection there?
- Do I have a 2nd degree connection (friend of a friend)?
- Have I talked to anyone from that company?
- Do they have a public Slack/Discord I could join?
- Have they posted content I could engage with?

**Step 3: Warm the lead before applying**

**Scenario A: You have a 1st degree connection**
```
Hey [Name],

Hope you're doing well! I saw you're at [Company]. I've been exploring product leadership roles and [Company] keeps coming up in my research.

I'd love to hear about your experience there. Would you have 20 minutes to chat?

Also, I noticed [Company] is hiring for [role]. After we chat, if you think I'd be a good fit, would you be open to referring me or introducing me to the hiring manager?

No pressure either way - would love to catch up regardless.

David
```

**Scenario B: You have a 2nd degree connection**
```
Hi [1st degree connection],

Quick question - I see you're connected to [Person] at [Company]. I'm exploring roles there and would love an introduction if you think it makes sense.

Here's a quick summary of what I do: [2 sentence pitch]

Would you be comfortable introducing us? Happy to send you a pre-written intro email to forward.

Thanks!
```

**Scenario C: No connection - Engage with content first**
- Follow the company on LinkedIn
- Comment thoughtfully on 2-3 of their posts over 2 weeks
- Then reach out to someone on the team:

```
Hi [Name],

I've been following [Company]'s work on [specific thing] and really impressed by [specific detail].

I'm currently exploring product roles and [Company] is at the top of my list. I'd love to learn more about the team and culture before applying.

Would you have 15 minutes to chat?

Thanks for considering.

David
```

**Step 4: Apply with context**

When you apply, mention the conversation in your cover letter:

```
I recently spoke with [Name] on your team about [topic]. After our conversation, I'm even more excited about [specific thing about the company/role].
```

This immediately separates you from the 200 other applications.

## Scripts for Common Situations

### "I don't know what to say on calls"

**Use this structure every time:**

**Minutes 0-5: Rapport**
- "Thanks for taking the time"
- "I've been at MOREgroup for 12 years and exploring what's next"
- "Tell me about your background"

**Minutes 5-15: Learning**
- "How did you get into [their role]?"
- "What do you like about [their company]?"
- "What's challenging about [their role]?"
- "What advice would you have for someone making this transition?"

**Minutes 15-20: The Ask**
- "Based on what I've shared, what roles do you think I should explore?"
- "Do you know anyone else I should talk to?"
- "Would you be open to introducing me to [specific person]?"

### "What if they say no?"

Most people will say yes to a 20-minute call. But if they don't:

```
No worries, I know you're busy! Is there anyone else you'd recommend I talk to about this?
```

Or just:

```
Totally understand. Thanks for considering!
```

And move on. You need 10 conversations, not 100.

### "What if I don't know what I want?"

That's okay. Say that:

```
I'm honestly still figuring out exactly what I want next. I've been building products for 12 years at the same company, and I'm exploring what else is out there. I'd love to learn about your path and what you've found fulfilling about [their role/company].
```

People respect honesty, and they'll often help you figure it out.

## The Mental Game

### Networking Feels Gross When:
- You only reach out when you need something
- You're not genuine about the connection
- You're treating people like transactions

### Networking Feels Good When:
- You're genuinely curious about people's work
- You offer value before asking for anything
- You build relationships over time
- You help others

**Reframe:** You're not "networking." You're having conversations with people who've done what you want to do.
